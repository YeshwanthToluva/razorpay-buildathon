03 · The policy engine, up close70s · same page

[Click into the boundary block. Click a few rule pills as you name them.]

This green block is the boundary. Fifteen rules, in a fixed order. First refusal wins. And the list ends in default-deny, so anything nobody wrote a rule for is refused.

[Click FRAUD_HOLD.]

Fraud hold. There is a fraud signal on the case, so no automated recovery happens at all until a person has reviewed it. The agent does not get a vote on that.

[Click MAX_RETRY_LIMIT.]

Max retry limit. The retry budget for this payment is spent. And this one is not just bookkeeping — repeated declines damage your acceptance rate with the issuer. Every future payment from that customer gets harder. So the rule protects something the agent cannot see.

[Click RETRY_COOLDOWN, then HIGH_VALUE_APPROVAL.]

Retry cooldown — too soon after the last attempt; back-to-back retries just get refused again. High value approval — above ten thousand rupees, a person has to sign off.

Every one of these is clickable, and every one opens in the rulebook.

[Scroll down the map: boundary → gateway → tools → ledger.]

And this is the shape of the whole thing. Model proposes. Policy judges. The gateway will not call anything without an AuthorizedAction — a type that can only be built inside the policy module. Then the tools. Then the ledger, which records all of it, including everything that was refused.

Q&A · backupnot part of the take

All data is synthetic. No real customer or payment data, and no real money moves — the payment rail is a simulator. The email is real.

The email allowlist holds one address and is enforced in the policy engine, not in the mail tool. An empty allowlist denies everyone, so a misconfigured deploy sends nothing.

Autonomy Level 2 — model proposes, deterministic policy disposes, no human in the loop. Escalation is terminal, which is why over-escalation cost the agent revenue. We measured it at 94.6% of the gap.

Why the agent lost: it escalates more than the rule set, and escalation closes a case at zero. We then improved its context, and its outcome feedback. Neither moved revenue — the second identical to the rupee. So the gap is not an information problem.

Simulator outcomes are a keyed hash of seed, payment, action and attempt, so a result never depends on the order cases ran in.
